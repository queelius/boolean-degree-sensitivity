# At most two negative quintics meet the core in exactly one vertex

In an actual signed geometry whose safe-pair graph on the positive quartet is
exactly a four-cycle, the two missing edges are disjoint diagonals. Each missing
edge is contained in at least one negative quintic, by the definition of safe.
One negative quintic cannot contain both diagonals: it would then contain every
core pair, including the four safe edges. Thus at least two distinct negative
quintics meet the core in two vertices. No negative quintic can contain three
core vertices, because every such triple contains a cycle edge.

There are four negative quintics in total. Therefore at most two meet the core
in exactly one vertex. Equivalently, the sum of negative flags across the four
singleton-core Fourier polynomials is at most two. This is stronger than the
unrestricted global budget of four for those four projections.

The independent actual-geometry audit checks the complete remaining signed
cover, constructs explicit frames for every four-cycle, verifies the two
disjoint sets of negative diagonal witnesses, and counts the singleton-core
negatives. This is a necessary budget for the actual four-cycle scope. It must
not be applied to an abstract graph where the other two pairs are also safe,
unless such cases have separately been excluded (as by the complete safe-star
theorem in the present accepted branch).
