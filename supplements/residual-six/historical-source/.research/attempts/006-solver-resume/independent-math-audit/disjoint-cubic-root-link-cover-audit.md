# Independent audit: disjoint cubic-exception root-link coverage

**PASS for structural coverage and the necessary ordinary-coefficient model.** This audit does not promote any probe outcome or exclude a derivative class. The scope is thirteen-variable ternary p with origin value1, all unit values0, constant Fourier numerator c in{1,2}, binary quartic numerators, ordinary quadratic coefficients0/1, and exactly two disjoint exceptional Fourier pairs: one -2 and the other -2 or+1. Special ordinary coefficient-two cases are outside this audit.

The implementation is `verify_disjoint_cubic_cover.py`; the report is `disjoint-cubic-root-link-cover-independent-verification.json`. It imports only prior independent-audit primitives, not producer or isomorphism-library code. No target solver or interval search was run.

Each triangle has a baseline pair and hence tetrahedron incidence zero or two. A negative endpoint meets exactly one exceptional edge, so the accepted one-six-edge splitting argument and projective-plane obstruction apply to its link using only its incident valences. Its link is a flag sphere of order9,10,11. A positive endpoint meets exactly one valence-three edge; the mixed-case curvature argument makes its link a sphere with one degree-three vertex and others of degree4/5, with order between7 and10.

Every remaining vertex needs a cross neighbor; negative endpoints need at least three and positive endpoints at least one. There are eight good vertices after removing the ordinary center. The other five vertices on its own side require five more cross edges. This is impossible. Thus all vertex links are spheres; minimum degree also gives connectedness.
