## 1. The six-vertex link lemma and positive tips

A triangulated S2 on six vertices cannot have exactly one vertex of degree3. Indeed, delete a degree3 vertex v and cap its triangular link. That cap triangle is absent beforehand: if already present, the star of v and that face form the entire boundary of a tetrahedron, a nonzero2-cycle in S2, forcing the whole sphere to have only four vertices. Deletion/capping therefore yields a genuine five-vertex sphere.

The latter has nine edges, so its graph is K5 minus one edge. Its two degree3 vertices are nonadjacent. Reintroducing v subdivides one triangular face; that face cannot contain both nonadjacent vertices. At least one old degree3 vertex stays degree3, in addition to v. This is the already accepted elementary1489 lemma, restated to expose every guard.

A positive tip has exactly one incident valence3 edge and all its other incident edges of valence4/5. Its sphere link therefore has exactly one degree3 vertex. Accepted minimum degree6 and the preceding lemma imply that its graph degree is at least7. No catalog is used.

Also, in a sphere link of order>4, two degree3 vertices cannot be adjacent. If they were, their two other common neighbors give all four boundary triangles of a tetrahedron, again a full top-dimensional2-cycle. Applied to the root link, the two positive tips at root degree7 are nonadjacent there. Thus the other positive tip is outside the three vertices of either positive tip's link.

## 2. Filled opposite triangle gives a genuine separating sphere

Fix a positive edge ab, with a the root and C_ab=boundary A, |A|3. Suppose A is a face of K. Subdivide ab stellarly, inserting m, obtaining a simplicial3-sphere K'. Each tetrahedron abxy of the old edge star is replaced by maxy and mbxy. All old edges remain except ab; the new edges join m to a,b and every vertex of A.

The four-set S={m} union A is a missing tetrahedron. Its four boundary triangles are A and the three mxy (xy edges of boundary A), all present. The tetrahedron mA is absent, because every new tetrahedron through m contains a or b. This construction uses only an edge subdivision and the filled face A; it does not assume a 3-to-2 move is legal.

Use the mod2 filling-chain partition for this missing tetrahedron, exactly as proved in1489, now on K'. Since H2(S3;F2)=0, its boundary2-sphere is the boundary of a3-chain. Tetrahedra receive its membership color. Every vertex outside S has a single color: all triangles through that vertex avoid the boundary, and its S2-link facet-dual graph is connected. The outside vertices split into sides P,Q, with no edge between sides in K'. No assertion that the sides are balls is needed.

The endpoints a,b lie on opposite sides. This detail is essential: for each xy in boundary A, the boundary triangle mxy has exactly the cofaces maxy and mbxy, and their colors are opposite. Thus the colors of a and b are opposite. Name the sides with a in P,b in Q. The ten outside vertices are exactly the original vertices V(K) minus A. Returning from K' to the original graph restores precisely the edge ab as its only P–Q edge.

## 3. Side sizes, cliques and exact endpoint degrees

Write p=|P|,q=|Q|, so p+q=10. For a nonendpoint vertex x in P, its original degree is at most (p-1)+3=p+2. The endpoint a has degree at most (p-1)+3+1=p+3, and similarly on Q. If a side has size1 or2, its endpoint degree is at most4 or5, contradicting minimum degree6. A side of size3 contains a nonendpoint, whose degree is at most5. Hence p,q>=4.

Both sides are cliques in the original graph. Were two P vertices nonadjacent, choose any Q vertex other than b (available since q>=4). The three would be independent, since the only cross edge is ab. This violates alpha<=2. The same reasoning applies to Q using a P vertex other than a. The exception ab is therefore handled explicitly, not accidentally included in a no-cross-edge premise about K.

Both a and b are adjacent to all three A vertices, because A is their edge-link support. With the clique and unique cross edge this gives the exact identities

    d(a)=p+3, d(b)=q+3, d(a)+d(b)=16.            (2)

For root degree7, p=4 and q=6.

## 4. The other positive tip creates the contradiction

There are two positive tips at root degree7. This follows from the inherited root profile: q_negative>=2 would require root degree>=8, whereas q_negative=1 gives two positive tips. Alternatively the actual accepted root7 source has exactly those two positive edges, checked below.

Call the other positive tip v. It differs from b and, by the nonadjacent-degree3 observation in the root S2-link, lies outside A. It is adjacent to a. Since ab is the only cross edge, v belongs to P minus{a}. With p=4, every nonendpoint in P has degree at most p+2=6. Minimum degree6 makes d(v)=6. But v is a positive tip, whose degree is at least7 by Section1. Contradiction.

Thus A is absent. Interchanging the two positive tips repeats the same reasoning, so both opposite triangles are missing. Interchanging names is bookkeeping, not a function automorphism assumption.

## 5. Root degree at least ten
