# Independent review: a missing triangle is necessary

Alexander Richard Towell, with substantial OpenAI Codex assistance.

**Conclusion:** the proposed1488 missing-triangle lemma is sound under its stated accepted1413/1453 premises. This is a conditional structural theorem. It excludes no native model, imposes no flagness, establishes no Boolean construction and changes no whole-pattern/global status. No model, propagation, SAT, filter or publication operation was performed.

Let K be the accepted connected triangulated S3 on13 vertices. Its ordinary edges have tetrahedral valence4/5, with exactly three exceptions at one root w: q negative edges of valence6 and3−q positive edges of valence3, where q∈{1,2,3}. Every induced subcomplex has Euler characteristic in[0,2]; the root graph degree is at least7,8,9 respectively. These are inherited geometric premises, not conclusions freshly proved here.

The argument below independently checks the original proof. The optional P-side simplification was supplied by the parent researcher and checked here; it is not claimed as independent rediscovery. The finite control code imports neither1488 nor a native constructor/catalog/solver.

## 1. Graph and link consequences with their actual guards

An independent triple in the graph of K would induce exactly three isolated vertices and characteristic3. Hence α(G(K))≤2.

Every vertex has graph degree at least6. Indeed, a non-root vertex has a triangulated S2 link with all degrees4/5 except possibly a single degree3 or degree6. A sphere needs at least4 vertices. At link order4 or5, even the smallest permitted total degree, `3+4(n-1)`, exceeds `6n-12`; a degree6 cannot improve this. Thus a non-root link has order≥6. The root already has order≥7.

A useful stronger fact is: **any vertex of graph degree≥9 lies in a missing triangle.** Its S2 link is a planar graph, so a four-coloring supplies an independent triple in that link. Since α(G(K))≤2, some pair from that triple is an edge in K. That edge is absent from the link, while both endpoint-root edges are present; their root triangle is therefore missing. No flag-sphere bound is applied to a nonflag object.

The four-color input is classical. The author-hosted primary paper [Chudnovsky–Nevo, *Stable sets in flag spheres*](https://web.math.princeton.edu/~mchudnov/flagstable.pdf), proof of Theorem2.7, PDF page8, explicitly uses four-coloring of a planar vertex link to obtain an independent set of at least a quarter of its vertices. Here only that planar coloring fact is used. Their general flag-sphere theorem is not asserted for K. No PDF body is redistributed.

## 2. If no triangle were missing, a tetrahedron would be missing

Assume for contradiction that no triangle is missing. Then the graph of every link `link_K(v)` is exactly the graph induced by K on the neighbors of v: an edge between two neighbors gives a3-clique with v and hence its triangle. By the preceding argument all graph degrees would be at most8. In particular q=3 is impossible because its root degree is at least9.

Thus q=1 or2 and a positive valence3 edge e exists. Its link is a triangular cycle on A={a,b,c}. The five vertices e∪A form a clique. All ten triangles on these vertices are present under the assumption. Three tetrahedra are exactly the star of e, `eab,eac,ebc`. If either of the two other four-sets is absent, it is already a missing tetrahedron. If both are present, K contains the entire boundary of a4-simplex.

That last possibility would make K the five-vertex boundary itself. One direct justification is that this boundary is a nonzero3-cycle over F2. A triangulated S3 has one-dimensional top-cycle space, generated by the sum of **all** its tetrahedra; there are no4-simplices. The subcycle must therefore be the full fundamental cycle. This contradicts thirteen vertices.

This also verifies the original minimal-nonface route: a nonflag3-complex with no missing triangle has a minimal nonface of size4 or5; a size5 one gives exactly the forbidden boundary component above. Flagness is not an assumption. The original q<3 nonflag argument via the triangular edge link is valid, as is its q=3 planar-link argument.

## 3. The separating partition, made explicit by a mod-2 chain

Let S be a missing tetrahedron on four vertices. Its four boundary triangles form an embedded2-sphere. The original geometric separation argument is valid for this finite simplicial sphere in S3. The following equivalent chain argument specifies all partition facts required below, without needing to claim that either side is a ball.

The boundary of S is a nonzero2-cycle over F2, and `H2(S3;F2)=0`. Choose a3-chain B whose boundary is exactly these four triangles; color tetrahedra in B with1 and the rest with0. Every triangle not in the boundary has two same-colored tetrahedral cofaces, and every boundary triangle has opposite-colored cofaces.

For any vertex v outside S, all incident tetrahedra have one color. Their adjacency through triangles containing v is the connected facet-dual graph of the S2 link of v; none of these triangles belongs to the boundary of S. Define P,Q as the outside vertices of colors0,1. Every outside vertex has exactly one color. Both colors have tetrahedra because the boundary is nonempty, and each such tetrahedron has an outside vertex because the tetrahedron S itself is absent. Thus P,Q are nonempty and together have nine vertices.

There can be no P–Q edge: every edge belongs to a tetrahedron, whose outside vertices all have its color. Since α(G(K))≤2 and the opposite side is nonempty, each of P,Q is a clique. An outside vertex on a side of size r has degree at most `(r-1)+4=r+3`. The minimum degree6 gives both sizes at least3. Up to interchange the only partitions are `(3,6)` and `(4,5)`.

Nothing here assumes that P or Q was originally connected, that sides are flag, or that a clique automatically gives a tetrahedron. Only triangles are filled under the contradiction assumption.

## 4. Both partitions contradict planar vertex links

### Partition3+6

Each vertex in the three-side must see its two side-mates and all four S vertices. The same applies to its side-mates, so its six neighbors induce a complete K6. Under no missing triangles, its link graph is K6, impossible for a planar sphere (15 edges exceed12).

### Partition4+5: streamlined P-side proof

Write |P|=4, |Q|=5, and for v∈P let `S_v⊆S` be its boundary neighbors. Since P is a clique, minimum degree6 gives |S_v|≥3.

If |S_v|=4, its seven neighbors have at least three edges among the other P vertices, six among S, and nine cross edges: every other P vertex has at least three S neighbors. That is18 edges, exceeding the seven-vertex planar bound15. Consequently every S_v has size3.

For any v∈P the six-neighbor link graph contains at least

`3+3+Σ_(u∈P\{v}) |S_u∩S_v|`

edges. Each intersection has size at least2, so the planar maximum12 forces every such intersection to be exactly2. The four3-subsets S_v must have distinct omitted vertices of the four-set S. In particular each s∈S misses a neighbor p∈P.

For every q∈Q, the pair p,q is already a nonedge by the partition. If s,q were also a nonedge, {s,p,q} would be independent. Hence **all S–Q edges exist**. A vertex q∈Q now has eight mutually adjacent neighbors: the other four Q vertices and all four S vertices. With no missing triangles its link graph is K8, with28 edges instead of the planar maximum18. Contradiction.

Thus no-triangle-missing is impossible. K must have a missing triangle.

## 5. Audit of the original six-vertex K4 lemma

The original1488 Q-side proof is independently sound too. A six-vertex triangulated S2 with no degree3 vertex has all degrees4 (sum24), so its graph is K6 minus a perfect matching, the octahedral graph, which has no K4. If a degree3 vertex exists, its opposite triangle cannot already be a face unless the entire sphere were the four-vertex tetrahedral boundary. Removing it therefore gives a five-vertex sphere. Such a graph has nine edges, so it is K5 minus one edge, the triangular bipyramid. The two degree3 vertices are nonadjacent; reinserting a vertex in one face can raise the degree of at most one of them. At least one old degree3 vertex survives alongside the new degree3 vertex. Therefore a six-vertex S2 containing K4 has at least two degree3 vertices.

Applied to a Q vertex with exactly two S neighbors, this would give at least two incident valence3 edges. Only the common root could have that property, but the root degree is at least7 and this vertex would have degree6. Hence all Q vertices have at least three S neighbors. The original17-edge/on7 and24-edge/on8 lower bounds then exceed15 and18. No six-vertex surface catalog, uniqueness classification beyond the elementary graph observations, or assumed flagness is needed.

The P-side proof above removes this auxiliary lemma from the main proof, but retaining its independent audit and controls helps prevent invalid generalizations.

## 6. Finite controls, limitations and scope

* All six face-stacks of the five-vertex triangular bipyramid produce six-vertex sphere controls containing K4 with at least two degree3 vertices. The octahedral control has no K4. The written degree3-removal/no-degree3 dichotomy supplies completeness of this small lemma; finite checks alone do not certify all labeled spheres.
* A face-stack of the octahedron is a genuine **seven**-vertex sphere containing K4 and exactly one degree3 vertex. This refutes extension of the auxiliary lemma beyond order6.
* Stacking one tetrahedron of the boundary4-simplex gives a small S3 with a missing tetrahedron. Its explicit mod-2 filling chain yields the two disjoint outside-vertex sides and no cross edge. Removing one filling tetrahedron is a failing chain-boundary control.
* The genuine eight-vertex flag sphere C4*C4 has no missing triangle, minimum graph degree6 and induced Euler range[0,2]. It is outside the thirteen-vertex/signed-root hypotheses. This prevents turning the result into an unguarded theorem about all such small spheres.
* The P-side count checks all625 assignments of3/4-subsets of a four-set to four vertices. Exactly24 survive all four local planar bounds, and they omit the four boundary vertices distinctly. The original Q-side count checks all3125 assignments and all15625 per-vertex inequalities; the minima are17 and24 for three/four S neighbors.
