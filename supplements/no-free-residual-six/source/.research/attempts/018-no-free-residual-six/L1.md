# L1: no derivative at a delta = 1 coordinate with at most one incident departure

Alexander Richard Towell, with Claude Code (Anthropic) assistance. Proposed
written lemmas plus an exact cover and certificates (`cases018.py`,
`cover_reader018.py`, `model018.py`, `certify018.py`, `reader018.py`).
Reviewed by Codex (REVIEW-L1-codex.md): sound after the fixes applied
here. Not frozen. Global status: OPEN.

## Setting

The empty-pair selfdual representative (1513 lines 7-9): f(0) = 0, f = 1
on every input of weight 1 or 2, h = 1 - 2f, k_S = 16 h_hat(S) at levels
1, 3, 5. Baseline: singleton k in {1,2}, cubic k in {-1,0}, quintic k in
{0,1}; negative quintics have magnitude m; every other departure costs at
least 2 (singleton 0 or 3, cubic -2 or +1, quintic 2) and the total
departure cost is the residual r.

Let u lie in exactly one negative quintic Q + u (delta(u) = 1). Then
p = Delta_u f on the other 13 coordinates satisfies 16p = k_u + sum_ab
k_uab chi_ab + sum_abcd k_uabcd chi_abcd, p in {-1,0,1}, p(0) = 1,
p(e_v) = f(e_u + e_v) - 1 = 0, ordinary quadratic coefficients
f(1_uab) in {0,1}; the quartic Q has numerator -m, and only departures
whose support contains u appear in p.

## Claim

For m in {1,2}: no such p exists when at most one residual departure
contains u and that departure (if any) has cost 2. (Every residual
departure costs 2 or at least 6; in (2^4) and (2^4,1) the residual is 4 or
2, so cost-6 departures cannot occur there.)

## Why this closes (2^4) and (2^4,1) (counting)

(2^4) has r = 4 (at most two cost-2 departures) and m = 2 on every
support; sum over coordinates of (delta - 1) is 5N - 14 = 6, so at least 8
coordinates have delta = 1. Two distinct departure supports share at most
4 coordinates, so at least 4 of them meet at most one departure. (2^4,1)
has r = 2 (at most one departure), m in {1,2}, and at least
14 - (25 - 14) = 3 coordinates with delta = 1. In both, union 14 is forced
(no-free-frontier.json). So L1 excludes both inventories outright.

## Lemmas used by the cover

1. Triangle incidence (for the types other than a +2 quartic, which item 4
   excludes first). For a triple T not inside Q every quartic containing
   T is unit, and T contains a pair outside Q that is not the departure
   pair (T has at least two pairs not inside Q). That pair's link is empty,
   C4 or C5, so the incidence of T is 0 or 2: t_T is binary. Inside Q,
   t_T = (-m + s)/2 with s the number of unit quartics T + y (y outside Q,
   so s <= 9), hence t_T is an integer in [-1, 6] (m = 1 forces s odd,
   m = 2 even); `model018.py` encodes it exactly as t + 2 t2 + 4 t4 - 1.
2. Ordinary vertices (outside Q and the departure support). Every pair
   through such a vertex v has a unit link (no quartic through v is Q),
   empty, C4 or C5, and P_v = 2 D_v - 4 holds with the weights, so the unit
   link of v is a union of closed surfaces with chi = 2 and degrees 4, 5:
   a sphere, hence one of the six deltahedra (017 NEAR-FLAG.md table, from
   the 015 enumeration), or two six-vertex projective planes (each
   component has chi >= 1; a chi = 1 component with degrees <= 5 has six
   vertices, all of degree 5). The identities give E = 49 + c also here.
3. Flag rows at ordinary vertices with a sphere link: the deltahedra are
   flag (017 `near_flag.py`).
4. +2 quartic departure D. D != Q, so D has a vertex x outside Q; x lies in
   no other departure, every pair through x is baseline, and the only
   non-unit quartic through x is D. The accepted private-vertex argument
   (`006/two-exception-positive-extension.md`, accepted in
   `006/independent-math-audit/connected-six-unit-exclusion-audit.md`,
   re-reviewed in 016) uses only these data at x and gives a unit link
   with Euler characteristic 3, impossible. So these types need no cover.
5. Pair departure with an endpoint x outside Q. Every pair through x except
   the departure pair has a unit link C4 or C5 (or empty); the departure
   pair's link is C6 or two triangles (-2) or C3 (+1); no non-unit quartic
   contains x. This is the setting of 017 VERTEX-TYPES.md Lemma 2 at x, so
   its link is one of the three type (1,0) spheres (orders 9, 10, 11; a
   lone split partner is impossible) or the type (0,1) sphere (order 7),
   with the partner y at its degree-6 or degree-3 vertex. A bowtie link of
   xy centred at z would put xyz in four tetrahedra, but xz is baseline,
   avoids Q and has a cycle link, so incidence at most 2. A lone split
   partner gives chi = 3 on at most 13 vertices; both degree-3 copies lie
   in a sphere component, which needs at least 8 vertices for their
   disjoint neighbourhoods, and the six-vertex projective plane makes 14.

## Cover

Fix a derivative of an allowed type. Either some ordinary vertex has a
disconnected link (part A: root there, link two six-vertex projective
planes on all twelve other coordinates; no flag rows), or every ordinary
link is a deltahedron. In the latter case root at an ordinary vertex
of maximum degree among the ordinary vertices (part B-ord) for the types
without a pair or with the pair inside Q (so every other ordinary vertex
has degree at most the root link's order: the `degcap` rows), or at
the pair's endpoint outside Q (part B-exc); flag rows at all other ordinary
vertices. Placements of Q and the pair are taken up to root-link
automorphisms and non-neighbour relabelling (`cases018.py`; 2221 cases;
`cover_reader018.py` checks every labelled placement).
